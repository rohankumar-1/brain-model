Reading it

  1. Where comparable results exist, ours is at or above them: ADNI (CN vs AD 0.918 vs 0.922 for a frozen 3D MAE), the ADNI 3-class task, IXI age, UCSF IDH and grade, RSNA ICH and the CQ500 macro.
  2. Against fully supervised references the gap is ~0.02-0.10 AUC on CQ500 (largest on subdural, epidural and skull fracture) with three findings at or above Chilamkurthy et al., whose model trained on ~290k labelled head CTs.
  3. Read by the model's own RSNA heads, with no CQ500 labels, every bleed finding beats the CQ500-fitted frozen readout (+0.02 to +0.07 AUC): IVH 0.981 is above the supervised reference, SAH and any bleed within 0.03. Refitting those heads on CQ500's labels never improved them; started from the head, one or two positives match what a from-scratch probe needs eight or more for.
  4. Segmentation is the clear gap: frozen tokens with a token-grid decoder reach 0.52-0.58 Dice against 0.69-0.82 for supervised models, and below the 20-case fine-tuned AMAES on WMH. Our tumour masks are far below single-sequence references.
  5. Several tasks are near chance for everyone (MGMT, PPMI, ISLES'24 outcome, EPVS count); an untrained encoder matches trained ones there, so they say little about representation quality.
  6. Many of our tasks have no published counterpart (bleed side, other fracture, LVO side, ATLAS lesion volume, MS lesion load, Pixar / MS3SEG age, aneurysm presence per modality).
  7. Caveats: published numbers use their own splits; test sets of 40-130 cases carry ±0.05-0.10 AUC intervals; several foundation models saw our evaluation cohorts in pretraining (noted per reference); "best earlier BQ" is the best of eight checkpoints, chosen with hindsight.
